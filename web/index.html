How should it be split?

Advanced

Not the wallet passphrase. It encrypts the shares, and recovery never reports a wrong one: it silently gives back a different phrase. Whoever recovers will need it exactly. Most backups should leave it blank and rely on the threshold.

PBKDF2 cost is 2500 × 2^exponent per round. 1 matches other SLIP-39 tools; it only matters with a SLIP-39 passphrase.

Before you start

You'll see one share at a time, and check each copy by typing back three of its words.

Keep the shares in different places, and make sure someone you trust knows they exist and how to recover. This page can print a "how to recover" sheet at the end.

What about the original?

Your phrase now exists twice: as these shares, and as the copy you started from. The shares protect you only as far as that original lets them: lose it and nothing is lost, but whoever finds it has your whole wallet.

Once you've tested recovery, you can destroy it, or keep it somewhere as safe as you'd keep any full backup. Your call: the shares work either way.

Print the shares

Until you separate the cards and store them in different places, the printout is your whole phrase. A home printer on your own network is fine; avoid office, shared and cloud printers. Don't choose "Save as PDF": a saved file is a full copy that can end up in cloud backups.

Skip checking your copies?

The checks catch a word copied wrong now, while it's easy to fix. Without them, a mistake may only show up when someone needs to recover, possibly years from now.
